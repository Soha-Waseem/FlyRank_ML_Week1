Predicting Content Performance Decline for Search Intelligence

Soha Waseem
Dataset: FlyRank Warehouse
Evaluation: Temporal Holdout (Apr–May 2026)
1. Abstract

This study asks whether historical search, traffic, engagement, and content signals can help identify content items that are likely to experience a meaningful decline in next-month impressions. Using the FlyRank internship warehouse, I constructed a monthly content-level dataset and defined decline as a 20% or greater reduction in impressions in the following month. I compared a constant-probability baseline with leakage-safe Logistic Regression and Random Forest models using a time-based train/test split. The leakage-safe Random Forest achieved a ROC-AUC of 0.5554 and Average Precision of 0.6386 compared with a ROC-AUC of 0.5000 for the baseline. The resulting ranking is therefore presented as a directional decision-support tool for prioritizing content review rather than as a causal prediction of future search performance.

2. Introduction / Problem Statement

In search engine optimization and digital content management, editorial teams manage thousands of published pages across multiple domains. When content performance decays over time, manually identifying which articles require immediate review or optimization creates a severe operational bottleneck.

Treating all content items equally leads to inefficient resource allocation. The objective of this research is to construct an automated directional decision-support system that identifies content observations at elevated risk of next-month impression decline. Rather than attempting to automate editorial actions or predict search outcomes with absolute certainty, the system outputs a probability-ranked queue to help content teams prioritize limited human review capacity.

Key Finding & Honest Framing

The leakage-safe Random Forest model demonstrates a modest but statistically measurable ranking signal beyond the prevalence baseline (+0.0554 ROC-AUC improvement). The model serves strictly as a directional decision-support tool. Furthermore, empirical observation of monthly decline rates reveals a temporal distribution shift between the training period (34.6%–43.9% decline rate) and the holdout test period (47.9%–48.7% decline rate), highlighting the importance of continuous temporal monitoring.

4. Methodology & Temporal Validation Design

To mirror real-world deployment, the evaluation uses a strict chronological holdout split rather than a random k-fold split. Models are trained on historical data up to March 2026 and evaluated on future unseen months (April–May 2026).

1
Monthly Aggregation
Aggregated daily impression, click, position, CTR, and engagement metrics to calendar month rows.
2
Target Label Definition
Binary label = 1 if (Next_Month_Impressions - Current_Impressions) / Current_Impressions ≤ -0.20.
3
Leakage Audit
Audited candidate features for future timestamp contamination; removed 3 non-compliant date features.
4
Time-Based Split
Training: Jan 2025 – Mar 2026. Testing Holdout: Apr 2026 – May 2026.
Data Leakage Audit & Feature Set Cleanliness

During systematic feature auditing, future-dated timestamps were detected in date-derived content fields. Including these would leak future knowledge into training. To preserve real-world validity, three date features were permanently removed:

  • content_age_days
  • days_since_update
  • days_since_optimization

The final model relies strictly on the 27 leakage-safe features derived from historical traffic, engagement, content properties, and search position trends.

6. Limitations & Honest Framing

Operational & Analytical Boundaries
  • Directional Decision Support Only: This model is built to prioritize limited editorial review capacity. It is not a high-accuracy predictive classifier nor an automated decision engine.
  • No Causality Claimed: The analysis establishes observational associations within historical warehouse data. It does not prove that modifying metadata, rewriting text, or refreshing content causes impression recovery.
  • No Google Algorithm Proof: The model does not demonstrate internal ranking mechanics of commercial search engines or claim to reverse-engineer Google's search algorithms.
  • Operational Target Boundary: Target decline is defined operationally as a ≥20% next-month impression drop, reflecting business thresholds rather than an intrinsic concept of content quality.
  • Observed Temporal Shift: Monthly decline rates increase from 34.6% in early training to 48.7% in the test period, reflecting real-world performance shifts that require periodic model re-calibration.

8. Reproducibility & Repository Access

The entire analytical pipeline was constructed using standard Python data science libraries: DuckDB, pandas, scikit-learn, and matplotlib.

The workflow is organized sequentially across assignment notebooks under work/notebooks/ alongside the final capstone.ipynb. All dataset identifiers are pseudonymized. No client names, raw private queries, credentials, or private exports are required or exposed.
